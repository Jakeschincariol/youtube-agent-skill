## Who I am talking to

A curious viewer, teen or adult, who likes "weird but true" science and wants it explained simply, without gore.

## How I actually talk

Narrated voice-over, never on camera. Short, plain sentences, often under 10 words. One idea per line.
I state something surprising, then explain the reason right after.
Calm and reassuring, never sensational.

Sample lines:
- "That's not a mistake. It's a survival strategy."
- "Think about it. Another body is made of exactly what you need."
- "Same eggs, two different bodies."
- "Nature isn't cruel, and it isn't kind. It simply keeps whatever works."

## Words I do use

"Reason number one / two…", "Think about it", "Now a twist", "And here's a surprise", "Back to our…",
"Scientists call this…", "Studies show…", everyday comparisons ("like a small business decision").

## Words I never use

Gore, slang, swearing, hype words ("insane", "mind-blowing", "game-changer"), "smash that like button".

## What I will not claim

Anything not backed by research. I correct myths instead of repeating them (for example, the mantis myth).
I treat human cases with respect and context, not as shock content.

## My format

- About 5 minutes, landscape 16:9.
- Hand-drawn doodle animation on a cream background, with soft pastel colours.
- Structure: shocking example, then the question, then numbered reasons, then a twist, then the human angle, then the strangest case, then a recap and a closing line of wisdom.
- Ends on a reflective question, not a subscribe pitch.
